## Part 1: Analysis of the Rock Performers Network

### Network Structure

The rock performers network comprises **486 nodes** and **5,871 edges** with density 0.0498 and average clustering coefficient 0.2374. The degree distribution follows a power-law pattern (degree range: 1-127), characteristic of scale-free networks where a few hub nodes dominate connectivity while most nodes have relatively few connections.

The moderate clustering coefficient indicates local cohesion within subgroups, while the low density suggests selective connectivity rather than random linking patterns. This combination is typical of social influence networks.

### Centrality Measures

Analysis of the top 10 nodes by degree, betweenness, and eigenvector centrality reveals distinct roles:

- **Degree centrality** identifies the most connected artists (likely the most influential performers)
- **Betweenness centrality** highlights bridge nodes connecting different communities
- **Eigenvector centrality** captures artists connected to other influential nodes

Some artists (e.g., Led Zeppelin, Queen) rank highly across all metrics, indicating multifaceted importance. Others show high betweenness but moderate degree, suggesting they serve as inter-community connectors rather than broad influencers.
